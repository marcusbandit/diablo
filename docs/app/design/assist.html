How the TCP handshake works

Before two machines send data, they agree on where each side starts counting. The client opens with SYN, the server answers SYN-ACK, and the client confirms with ACK.

Only then does the connection carry data. A lost SYN is sent again after a timeout, and the client gives up after a few tries.

Each side keeps its own sequence numbers, picked at random so an old packet from an earlier connection is not mistaken for a new one.

Closing takes four messages: each side says FIN and the other confirms it.

Three messages, sir. Each side says where it starts counting and confirms the other's number. Shall I draw it?
